# Competitive Analysis

> The goal is not to know what competitors do — it's to understand where they systematically fail a segment you could serve better.

---

## The core question

Competitive analysis is not about feature comparison. It's about understanding:
1. **What jobs are users hiring solutions for today?**
2. **Where do current solutions fall short for specific segments?**
3. **Where is the market heading — and what will incumbents be slow to respond to?**

---

## What to analyze

### 1. Direct competitors
Products explicitly targeting the same job as you.

For each:
- What segment are they primarily serving?
- What's their primary differentiation?
- Where do they systematically underperform?
- What do churned users say?

**Where to find signal:** G2, Capterra, App Store reviews. Focus on **3-star reviews** — 5-star is love, 1-star is noise, 3-star is where you find honest trade-offs.

### 2. JTBD alternatives
What users do *instead of your product* — not necessarily a direct competitor.

Example: for a project management tool, the real alternative might be a spreadsheet, not Asana. Understand why spreadsheets persist despite "better" tools existing. That tension is where insight lives.

### 3. Adjacent players
Products that don't compete today but could expand into your space — or whose user base overlaps with yours. These are the strategic risks to watch.

### 4. Market trends
- What's changing in the underlying technology?
- What regulatory or economic shifts are in play?
- Where are incumbents structurally slow to respond? (Large customer base they can't disrupt, business model constraints, legacy architecture, org structure)

---

## The Incumbent Gap Analysis

The most strategically useful output of competitive analysis.

**The pattern:** incumbents systematically underserve specific segments because:
- Their product was built for a different customer archetype (enterprise product poorly adapted for SMBs, or vice versa)
- Business model incentives prevent them from serving the segment profitably
- Legacy architecture constraints make fast iteration impossible
- Organizational inertia — the segment is too small to prioritize given their existing customer base

**The question to answer:** Which segment has the biggest gap between their actual need and what incumbents offer? That gap is where strategy starts.

This is the bridge between competitive analysis and the strategy layer (Where to Play + How to Win).

---

## How to do it well

1. **Map the JTBD first** — understand the job before comparing features. Feature gaps are meaningless without knowing what job they're supposed to do.
2. **Segment users** — different segments have very different requirements. Don't analyze "the market" as a monolith.
3. **Read the reviews** — especially churned user reviews. 3-star reviews are the highest-signal input you have without talking to users.
4. **Use the product yourself** — for any competitor you're seriously analyzing, do real tasks with it.
5. **Talk to switchers** — people who switched from a competitor to you (or vice versa) are the most informative source you have. Ask: what was the moment they decided to switch?

---

## Anti-patterns

- **Feature matrix as strategy** — a table of checkboxes tells you nothing about where you can win
- **Analyzing the competitors you fear, not the ones your users compare you to** — these are often not the same
- **Snapshot analysis** — competitive landscape shifts. One analysis doesn't stay valid. Build it into ongoing discovery.
- **Assuming parity is the goal** — matching a competitor on every dimension is not a strategy, it's catch-up. You can't win by being slightly better at everything.

---

## Key questions

- Who are users comparing us to when they decide — and why?
- What do the 3-star reviews of our main competitors say about their unmet needs?
- Where does the incumbent's business model prevent them from serving our target segment well?
- What would have to change for a large player to enter our space?
- Which segment is most underserved by what currently exists?
