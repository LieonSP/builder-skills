# Document Preparation — Backward Planning Method

Source: observed from a former manager at Amazon. A discipline that consistently produced polished, well-aligned documents on the day of presentation.

---

## The core idea

Work backward from the presentation date. Build alignment progressively — structure first, then content, then stakeholder buy-in — so that on D-day there are no surprises and no last-minute rewrites.

---

## The 6-week timeline

| Milestone | What's ready |
|-----------|-------------|
| **D-6 weeks** | Document structure locked — sections, headers, narrative arc |
| **D-5 weeks** | Key bullet points populated inside the structure — the skeleton of each section |
| **D-4 weeks** | First full draft — rough but complete |
| **D-3 weeks** | V1 ready to share with the first reviewers (VPs, key stakeholders) |
| **D-2 / D-1 week** | Revised version incorporating feedback from reviewers |
| **D-day** | Final, polished, rehearsed — no open questions |

---

## Stakeholder alignment — the hidden layer

Reviewers are not just VPs. Before the document reaches its audience, it typically needs validation from multiple people — peers, cross-functional leads, sponsors. This alignment can happen:

- **In a common forum** — a working session or review meeting with all stakeholders present
- **In separate 1:1s** — individual pre-reads, especially useful when stakeholders have conflicting views that need to be resolved before the room

The rule: **no one should be surprised in the room**. Every key stakeholder should have seen, questioned, and implicitly endorsed the content before D-day.

---

## Why this works

- **Structure precedes content** — deciding what to say before saying it prevents rewrites that touch everything
- **Feedback is absorbed progressively** — one round of comments at V1, not five rounds the week before
- **Alignment is built, not requested** — stakeholders who shaped the doc will defend it; those who saw it for the first time in the room often won't
- **D-day is a performance, not a draft** — the presenter is confident, not catching up

---

## When to use it

Any document with high stakes and multiple stakeholders:
- Leadership reviews, strategy presentations
- PRDs or roadmaps going to exec sign-off
- Quarterly business reviews, OKR check-ins
- Any cross-functional doc requiring alignment before the meeting

---

## PM trap to avoid

Starting the draft at D-3 weeks and doing stakeholder alignment at D-1. This compresses the feedback loop and almost always produces a weaker document — or a stressful D-day.
